We used a fixed stratified 80/20 split. Five-fold cross-validation was confined to the training data. Numeric preprocessing and imbalance handling were fitted separately inside each fold. SMOTENC resampled primitive COD and categorical route; derived ratios and zero flags were reconstructed afterwards.

1:30-1:55 | CV comparison table
We compared a dummy baseline with logistic regression, a decision tree, a random forest, histogram gradient boosting, XGBoost, and a random forest with SMOTENC. Model selection used mean validation average precision, not test accuracy. The selected model was Random forest with SMOTENC. Its validation average precision was 0.208; differences from other leading models were small.

1:55-2:30 | Holdout table and confusion matrix
The review threshold, 0.5164, was chosen from training out-of-fold F1 and frozen before holdout evaluation. On 864 holdout orders, average precision was 0.245, ROC-AUC 0.585, precision 22.5 percent, recall 24.4 percent, and F1 0.234. The flag identified 29 returns, also flagged 100 successful orders, and missed 90 returns. High majority-class accuracy alone therefore does not demonstrate useful return detection.
